# South Korean hearing-aid reimbursement and disability registration

Run `python3 standardize.py` from any directory. Python's standard library is sufficient. The script writes `grade_standardization.csv` next to the source. The included inputs are a transcription of published counts and rates, with no insurer microdata.

Hayoung Byun, Eun Mi Kim, Inah Kim, Seung Hwan Lee, and Jae Ho Chung (2022), “The trend in adoption of hearing aids following changes in provision policy in South Korea,” *Scientific Reports* 12, 13389. [Publication and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-17459-7). [Open author-affiliated PDF](https://hanyang.scholarworks.kr/api/assets/87fdab56-2fed-4be2-ab37-0850d95029c5/download).

## Input definitions

Table 2 (p. 3) reports registration counts by disability grade. Figure 4A–C (p. 6) prints percentages with a subsidized hearing aid by the end of observation, by registration year and severity. Panel A is all grades. Panel B is grades 4–6, and panel C is grades 2–3. The combined integer counts give the two category weights. Rates are in percent. The study selected NHIS registrants aged 30–80 and excluded medical-aid recipients, people with multiple disabilities and records missing major information. Its definition of acquisition requires a hearing-aid prescription and a subsidy payment observed during 2004–18.

Effective 15 November 2015, the hearing-aid benefit reference amount increased from KRW 340,000 to KRW 1,310,000. The Ministry of Health and Welfare announced the change on 12 November. This is the reference amount for computing reimbursement. The individual benefit depends on the eligible purchase price and coverage rate. No benefit amount enters the cohort standardization. [Government announcement](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=148803050). We display two calendar cohorts on either side and the transition cohort. The main comparison uses 2014 and 2016, the two adjacent complete calendar years. All four possible pre/post contrasts in this five-year window are tabulated. The full publication also shows earlier cohorts and the terminal 2018 cohort, which had the shortest observation window. We use integer counts directly throughout, avoiding the units ambiguity in the published population-rate label.

## Calculation and scope

Holding the proportion in grades 2–3 at its pre-period level, apply the two published group rates to the same baseline weights in both years. The weighted change measures the change within the two broad recorded severity strata among newly certified people. Each printed rate can differ from its unrounded value by at most 0.05 percentage points. Since fixed weights sum to one, the conservative rounding interval around a two-cohort difference is ±0.10 percentage points. This interval measures display precision only. It contains no sampling or causal uncertainty. Rates from Figure 4A are independently checked against the weighted rates reconstructed from B/C, with tolerance for reported rounding.

The two-grade standardization does not account for age, severity differences within grades, application and certification incentives, medical-aid exclusions, secular trends, other access changes or differential time from registration to the end of observation in 2018. The national policy lacked an untreated geographic comparison in this study. The unit is a registration cohort, not a purchase year, and paid claims do not establish first-ever receipt, wearing, clinician time or congestion among incumbent patients. The calculated change is descriptive and cannot calibrate an adoption elasticity.
