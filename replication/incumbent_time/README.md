# Incumbent encounter time following patient reassignment

Input is transcribed from Jori Barash, *Do New Patients Displace Existing Patients’ Treatment?*, June 2025 working paper, Table 3, printed p. 15, columns 1–6. The input includes all three provider splits on the reported time outcome. Source PDF: https://joribarash.github.io/Crowdout.pdf . The PDF page images were checked against the entries. Coefficients and physician-clustered standard errors are rounded to three decimal places in the paper. The source study instruments physician enrollment with administrative patient reassignments. The outcome is monthly encounter hours summed across the fixed incumbent cohort at a physician. Never interpret the outcome as time per individual incumbent.

Run `python3 compare_hours.py` to reconstruct `contrast_intervals.csv`. Python standard library only. Each contrast is first group minus second group, converted from hours to minutes per extra registration per month across that physician's incumbent patients. No registry microdata are included or claimed to be re-estimated.

Inference uses the asymptotic Wald approximation underlying the subgroup estimates. For the difference, Cauchy–Schwarz implies SE at most SE(first) + SE(second) for any covariance. The intervals use this upper bound. Each printed SE is inflated by 0.0005 and each end of the interval is expanded a further 0.001 hours for possible rounding error in the two coefficients. The two-sided critical value uses Bonferroni protection across all three provider splits (familywise 95% coverage under the Wald assumptions). Intervals incorporate sampling and rounded transcription, not bias from the original design. These are secondary comparisons chosen after reading the study. They do not adjust over studies, outcomes, or all possible subgroup investigations.

Full time means at least six encounter hours per weekday on average in the six months preceding reassignment. The ceiling comparison uses enrollment over the study spell, including the period exposed to reassignment, so cannot identify the causal moderating effect of starting capacity. Baseline schedule subgroups can have other underlying differences and potentially different IV complier populations. Assignment does not randomize schedule, panel ceilings, nor capacity. These results concern a local primary-care enrollment shock, not a device subsidy, audiology aftercare, or welfare.

The underlying study requires continuous registration with the incumbent physician across all 13 event months, including the six post-event months. Effects for patients who leave are outside this sample. The assignment design also requires that reassignments affect incumbent outcomes through enrollment after the included fixed effects.
