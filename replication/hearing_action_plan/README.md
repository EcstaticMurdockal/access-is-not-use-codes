# Reanalysis of hearing-aid use following randomized action planning

Ismail AH, Armitage CJ, Munro KJ, Marsden A, Dawes PD. Evaluation of the I-PLAN intervention to promote hearing aid use in new adult users. Ear and Hearing 43 (2022), 1103–1113. DOI https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000001195. Open primary report https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9197143/ (CC BY 4.0).

Table 3 gives the four assignment denominators. Table 4 supplies the observed arm sizes, means, and standard deviations in arms.csv. The self-report measure has five response values from 0 to 100 in 25-point steps. Hours per day come from the device logger. Each group received usual care and written information. The separately randomized components encouraged writing an action plan and using the hearing-aid box as a reminder. The materials were handed out at the end of the initial fitting consultation. Outcomes were assessed six weeks later. The randomized factors did not change the assigned duration of professional follow-up.

Run from the archive root with Python 3 and SciPy installed:

    python replication/hearing_action_plan/reanalyze.py

The script regenerates contrasts.csv, tipping.csv and the five displayed contrasts in contrasts_table.tex. Each marginal contrast assigns equal weight to the two levels of the other randomized factor. The interaction is the difference between the plan effects with and without the prompt. Observed-case pointwise intervals use the four independent arm means and sample standard deviations, with a Welch–Satterthwaite approximation. Rounded questionnaire means yield unique unrounded arm totals on the questionnaire grid. For logged hours the point estimates use the published means. The paper's original analysis adjusted for age, sex, hearing thresholds and baseline handicap. The published adjusted interaction test on logged hours was F(1,203) = 5.13, p = 0.03. This test is reported from the article. From the four rounded arm means the plan changed logged hours by +1.50 without the reminder and −1.60 with it, giving an equal-weight mean of −0.05 and a with-minus-without interaction of −3.10 hours/day. The assignment contrasts and Welch intervals computed here are unadjusted.

The completion endpoints use all 60 assignments per cell. They allow every unobserved questionnaire response any admissible value from 0 to 100, and every missing log any value from 0 to 24 hours. Logged-hour endpoints also encompass rounding of the published cell means to the nearest tenth. These are deterministic bounds on the full-assignment sample contrast under arbitrary completion. Population uncertainty is additional. The observed-case confidence intervals condition on the available measurements, and informative attrition can affect them. The secondary contrasts have no multiplicity correction.

This trial exposes self-report and logged duration to the same randomized service components. The outcomes have different denominators and units: targeted use during hearing difficulty and all-day logger hours. A difference in their responses does not establish reporting bias. The trial did not assign a provider-pool capacity shock or measure existing patients' spillovers.

The tipping.csv output uses the questionnaire's discrete scale to recover observed sums by plan assignment. Across all 120 participants assigned per factor level, the contrast equals (1175 + 15m1 - 18m0)/120 percentage points, where m1 and m0 are the mean questionnaire scores of missing respondents under plan and no plan. Even if every missing no-plan response were 100, a nonpositive full-assignment contrast would require a mean of 41.67 or less among missing plan respondents. This is a sensitivity threshold for the observed trial assignments.
