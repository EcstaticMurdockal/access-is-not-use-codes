# What part of the extra hearing-clinic workload can we observe?

Older U.S. veterans eligible for free hearing aids were individually randomized to one of three hearing-screening methods or usual care. People using hearing aids at baseline and people assessed in the past six months were excluded. In all arms, participants could contact audiology. Yueh and colleagues randomized 2,305 people. A companion analysis by Liu and colleagues removed the 54 who died during follow-up and reported service use among 2,251 survivors. We use only that companion paper’s Table 3. The contrast below therefore compares randomized arms among survivors. Differences between survivor composition and the original randomized population remain possible.

Over the year, combined questionnaire and tone screening was associated with about **21 additional audiology visits per 100 survivors** compared with no screening (approximate 95% interval 11.6 to 30.4). About **15.7 more people per 100** attended at least once (11.1 to 20.3). Subtracting those first visits leaves **5.3 additional later visits per 100** (−1.1 to 11.7). The last figure is imprecise. A later visit can be another assessment, a fitting or follow-up. Records available in the table do not distinguish these purposes. A person’s first visit here means the first within the observation year, and some participants could have visited before enrollment. Any visit received outside VA captured by the study also contributes.

This contrast attributes greater demand for audiology among participants to a screening offer in a system with no-charge devices. A first-fitting subsidy was not randomized. No existing hearing-aid users were followed. Months, minutes, costs of old users' lost care, and changes to queues cannot be inferred from these aggregate data. Other trials and aggregate sources cannot convert this result into an estimate of those missing outcomes. The arm-level means, standard deviations and proportions are transcribed from the publication. The separation of incremental visits and calculation of standard errors are secondary analyses of published statistics.

## Reproduce

Run `python decompose_visits.py` (Python 3.9+, standard library). The inputs are `arm_data.csv`. All three trial arms versus the same usual-care group appear in `visit_contrasts.csv`. The 95% intervals are approximate, unadjusted, two-sided, uncorrected for multiple comparisons and calculated from independent arm-level moments without adjustment for blocking. The combined arm was chosen here to describe the full screening package. Percent using an aid at one year is in the input for context. It is not a visit or receipt endpoint and is not used to infer counts.

For visits per person N and the first-visit indicator I = 1(N > 0), the number of visits beyond the first is R = N − I. Since N × I = N, their sample covariance is n × mean(N) × (1 − mean(I)) / (n − 1). This permits a variance estimate for R without assuming that having any visit and the number of visits are independent. Differences are multiplied by 100 for presentation. Rounding sensitivity enumerates integer counts of total encounters and people with any visit compatible with the published means and percentages and varies each supplied standard deviation by 0.005. Integer constraints are not imposed on second moments, so the resulting ranges conservatively enclose rounding ambiguity under the stated scoring assumptions. Intervals remain indicative rather than exact randomization inference.
