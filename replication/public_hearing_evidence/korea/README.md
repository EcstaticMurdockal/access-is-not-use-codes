# South Korea · hearing-aid use before and after the 2015 expansion

This secondary analysis uses the percentages in Table 4 and its accompanying Results paragraph in *Temporal Trends in Hearing Aid Utilization in Korean Adults: A KNHANES-Based Study (2010–2022)*, Journal of Korean Medical Science (2025). [Peer-reviewed source and table](https://doi.org/10.3346/jkms.2025.40.e289). [Publisher PDF, Table 4 on page 8/11](https://jkms.org/Synapse/Data/PDFData/0063JKMS/jkms-40-e289.pdf).

Run `python compare_usage.py` with Python 3 from this directory. The script reads `source_cells.csv` and writes `usage_contrasts.csv` alongside it. Each input is a published weighted percentage, rounded to two decimals. The authors report 10,019 included survey participants aged 40 or older during 2010–12 and 9,994 during 2020–22. Those are survey-wide counts. The much larger values printed beside the percentages in the source table are population-weighted projections, and cannot serve as sample sizes for confidence intervals.

Within the study's classification, high-threshold eligibility requires at least 60 dB hearing loss in the better ear. Another eligible group has at least 40 dB in the better ear and at least 80 dB in the poorer ear while falling below the high-threshold group. The comparison group has disabling hearing loss of at least 40 dB but meets neither support threshold. These categories are based on measured audiometry. Participants' actual subsidy receipts are unavailable. The source labels its oldest row “70s” and describes its top bin as “70+” in Methods.

**Results.** The increase in the prevalence of hearing-aid use was 30.46 percentage points in the high-threshold group and 5.19 points in the comparison group. Subtracting yields 25.27 points. Within the three age categories the analogous differences are 10.90, 22.29 and 28.41 points. The two groups started at very different usage levels. Expressed proportionally, the high-threshold prevalence grew 2.32-fold while comparison prevalence grew 2.23-fold. The ratio of their growth factors is just 1.04. A common additive trend and a common proportional trend therefore have quite different implications. Neither trend assumption has been established.

**Interpretation.** These are repeated cross-sections ten years apart. Individuals may enter the survey pool with aids they already owned. New purchases, replacements, month of fitting, financing source, clinic and follow-up minutes are unobserved in these percentages. Hearing severity, composition and prevalence of other conditions can change differently across groups, and audiometer and middle-ear assessment procedures changed. Age stratification addresses a specific compositional concern but does not establish causation. Source Table 4 gives no survey-design standard errors or covariances for these new contrasts. Its published tests apply to other comparisons. This is evidence of real-world hearing-aid use, separate from claims among people choosing to register for hearing disability.
